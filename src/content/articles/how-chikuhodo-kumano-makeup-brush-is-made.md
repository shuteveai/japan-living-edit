A makeup brush is easy to understand as a finished object. The manufacturing process is less obvious.

CHIKUHODO's official [brush-crafting process](https://www.chikuhodo.com/en/company/process.html) is useful because it breaks the work into named stages: selecting and blending hair, removing unsuitable hairs, shaping the brush tip, tightening the bundle, massaging, attaching the metal fitting, and installing the shaft.

This article is not a comparison between CHIKUHODO and HAKUHODO.

It is a look at what actually happens during one documented Kumano makeup-brush process.

## 1. Start With the Hair

The process begins before the brush has a recognizable shape.

CHIKUHODO describes using carefully selected raw hair, with different types of hair having different hardness, thickness, and characteristics.

That is a material decision.

It does not mean natural hair is automatically better than synthetic fiber. It means the company treats hair type and hair condition as inputs that affect the finished object.

For an everyday tool, that matters because the visible brush head begins as a set of material choices.

## 2. Selection and Blending

CHIKUHODO's first documented stage is "Select hair/hair blends."

The company explains that different raw hairs may be used in blends for different functions and uses, and to create consistent quality.

This gives a more concrete meaning to the word "craft."

Craft is not only final assembly. It begins with deciding which material belongs in the brush and how different hairs should be combined.

That is a functional decision, not just a tradition.

## 3. Removing Hairs That Do Not Belong

The second stage is described as "Fine hair (cutting of puffed up hair)."

CHIKUHODO says that puffed-up hair and damaged hair ends are removed upward using half cutters.

This is a small detail, but it reveals something important.

The brush head is not simply gathered and tied. It is edited.

Material that does not fit the intended brush tip is removed before the final shape is completed.

That does not prove a particular tactile experience for every user.

It does show that the visible softness or shape of a brush depends on earlier sorting and correction.

## 4. The Shape Is Formed

The third stage is "Frame-insertion."

CHIKUHODO describes inserting hair into a frame to form the brush tip. The frame is also made according to the intended use of the makeup brush.

This is where shape becomes more than appearance.

Brush shape affects how the tool is meant to interact with powder, skin, edges, and areas of the face. The official process does not require us to claim that one shape is universally better.

It does show that the shape is built into the process rather than added as decoration.

For broader context on evaluating makeup-brush construction, see our guide to [HAKUHODO makeup brushes](/articles/what-makes-hakuhodo-makeup-brushes-different/).

## 5. Thread Holds the Bundle Together

After frame insertion, CHIKUHODO describes "Thread-tightening."

A wire is wrapped around the base of the hairs before they are removed from the frame. The company describes this as a precise process that requires an appropriate amount of strength for the next stage.

That gives the brush head temporary structure before metal and handle components are added.

The finished brush may look seamless, but the process depends on intermediate steps that hold the material in place.

## 6. What Does "Massaging" Mean Here?

The next stage is "Massaging."

CHIKUHODO describes this as a process used to increase the integrity of the brush tip created during frame insertion. The brush-crafter decides where and how to massage the hairs based on the image of the completed tip and how the hairs feel.

This is handwork, but it should not be turned into a vague promise that handmade automatically means better.

The useful point is more specific.

The company identifies a manual adjustment stage after the brush tip has been formed, before the metal fitting and handle complete the object.

## 7. Ferrule and Handle Complete the Brush

CHIKUHODO then describes cutting excess hairs at the base of the completed brush tip, fastening the metal fitting, and installing the shaft.

These steps turn the shaped hair bundle into a complete brush.

The ferrule and handle are not only visual parts.

They hold the brush tip, give the tool a usable structure, and complete the transition from material bundle to handheld object.

As with other everyday tools, small assembly decisions can affect what the object is meant to do.

## 8. What Handwork Does, and Does Not, Tell Us

CHIKUHODO's process includes many hand-performed stages.

That is meaningful information.

But handwork does not automatically prove durability, comfort, performance, or superiority over every other brush.

It tells us how the object is made.

That is already useful.

It lets us see the brush as a sequence of material and process decisions: select, remove, shape, tighten, adjust, fasten, and assemble.

## Final Thoughts

The CHIKUHODO process shows that a makeup brush is not just a soft head attached to a handle.

It is built through decisions about hair, blending, removal, shaping, thread tightening, manual adjustment, metal fitting, and shaft installation.

None of those steps requires a competitive ranking.

They simply make the object easier to understand.

When evaluating a brush, the useful question is not whether it is handmade in the abstract.

It is which parts of the process shape the tool you eventually use.
