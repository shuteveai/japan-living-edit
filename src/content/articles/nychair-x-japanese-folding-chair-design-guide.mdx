A folding chair can be easy to dismiss as temporary furniture.

It can suggest a spare seat, a guest chair, or something stored away until there is no better option. But some folding furniture deserves a slower reading because the folding structure is not just a convenience. It is part of the product's design logic.

The [Nychair X](https://www.nychairx.jp/nychairx/) is useful to examine for that reason. It is a Japanese folding chair with a design lineage that dates to 1970, current production by FUJIEI, Made-in-Japan manufacturing information, and a support ecosystem that includes replacement parts and a repair program in Japan.

This is not a hands-on review. It is a fact-based product guide for overseas readers who want to understand what can be verified before deciding whether the exact NY-125 fits their space, expectations, and support needs.

The useful question is not whether the Nychair X is iconic, comfortable, or better than another chair.

**It is what the chair's folding structure, dimensions, origin, replaceable parts, and repair limitations actually mean for ownership.**

## 1. What Is the Nychair X?

The Nychair X is a folding chair designed by Takeshi Nii.

FUJIEI's current official site describes it as the original model in the Nychair X series. The product is associated with an X-shaped leg structure, wooden armrests, and a seat fabric that forms the sitting surface.

For Japan Living Edit, the chair is interesting because it connects several practical questions at once.

It is furniture, but it folds. It has a long production history, but the current product should still be evaluated as a current model. It has replacement parts, but that does not mean unlimited repairability. It is made in Japan, but origin alone is not a quality conclusion.

That makes it a useful first furniture entry in a Made in Japan Product Guide: specific enough to study, but familiar enough that the ownership questions are easy to understand.

## 2. Why a Folding Chair Can Be More Than Temporary Furniture

Folding furniture is often judged by whether it can disappear when not in use.

That is only part of the story.

A folding chair still has to work when open. It needs enough floor space, enough clearance around it, a place to be stored, and a reason to move between open and folded states. If those details do not fit the home, folding alone does not solve the space problem.

Nychair X is relevant to small-space thinking because it asks the buyer to consider both states:

- the open chair as a place to sit
- the folded chair as an object that must still be stored somewhere

That two-state evaluation is similar to the broader principle in our guide to [Japanese storage ideas for small apartments](/articles/japanese-storage-ideas-small-apartments/): storage and furniture work best when they follow the real behavior of the home.

The point is not that a folding chair is automatically space-saving.

The point is that movement and storage are part of the product.

## 3. The Exact Model: Nychair X NY-125

The exact product discussed in this article is the Nychair X NY-125.

Amazon Japan identifies the product as model NY-125, with dimensions of 61 x 76.5 x 86 cm, weight of 6.5 kg, and seat height of 25.5 cm. The official Nychair X product page gives corresponding approximate dimensions of W 610 x D 765 x H 860 mm and seat height of approximately 255 mm.

Those numbers are central to the buying decision.

The chair may fold, but when open it still occupies a real footprint. Before considering it for a small apartment, compare the open dimensions with the intended placement area. Then compare the folded object with the place where it would actually be stored.

Useful measurements include:

- the current chair or lounge chair it might replace
- the floor area available when the chair is open
- circulation space around the chair
- the doorway, closet, wall, or corner where it might be stored
- whether moving it between those places would be part of normal use

The exact model matters because different Nychair X series products have different proportions.

## 4. Designed in 1970 — and Still in Production

Nychair X's official history states that the chair was released in 1970 by Nii Furniture after being created by designer Takeshi Nii. The same history explains that Nii Furniture announced the end of chair manufacturing in 2013, and that FUJIEI took over the Nychair business and released FUJIEI-manufactured Nychair X products from 2014.

That history is meaningful, but it needs careful interpretation.

It supports saying that the design lineage has continued for more than 50 years. It does not, by itself, prove that every individual chair will last for decades. It also does not mean the current NY-125 is technically identical in every detail to a chair manufactured in 1970.

For an editorial product guide, the long history is most useful as context.

It shows that the chair is not a newly invented seasonal product. The more important question for a buyer remains current and practical:

**Does the present model, with its present dimensions, parts, materials, and support limits, fit your home?**

## 5. What “Made in Japan” Means Here

Made in Japan is a verified part of the product's identity.

FUJIEI's official FAQ states that, for FUJIEI-made Nychair X products, the origin of the leg-related components and fabric is Japan, and that assembly and sewing are carried out at domestic factories with relevant production experience. Amazon Japan also identifies the NY-125 product's country of origin as Japan.

That supports describing the current NY-125 as Made in Japan.

It should not be turned into a shortcut for quality claims.

Manufacturing origin is a verifiable fact. It tells the reader where this product is made and how the official manufacturer describes part of its production. It does not replace questions about dimensions, use case, maintenance, parts availability, or overseas support.

That is the same editorial distinction Japan Living Edit uses when looking at other Made-in-Japan products: origin matters, but it is one part of the product's identity rather than a conclusion by itself.

## 6. A Chair Built with Replaceable Parts

The current official Nychair X online shop lists replacement seats and parts for Nychair X series products.

Current categories include replacement seats, armrest and leg-related sets, seat pipes, screws and nuts, care brushes, and other parts. The support FAQ also describes examples where parts such as bolts, washers, and nuts can be replaced.

That matters because it shows the chair is not treated only as a sealed object.

Some parts can be bought and replaced, and FUJIEI provides support information for assembly and seat replacement. That can make ownership more understandable, especially for a chair made from visible components rather than a fully upholstered shell.

But replaceable parts should be interpreted carefully.

Parts availability can change. Compatibility can depend on the manufacturing company, product generation, model, and fabric or frame details. FUJIEI's FAQ notes that replacement seats, screws, and seat pipes made by the company correspond only to specified Nychair X products.

Replacement parts are therefore a practical support feature, not a promise that every component will always be available.

## 7. What Repairability Does — and Does Not — Mean

Nychair X's official support ecosystem includes both replacement parts and a repair service in Japan.

That is meaningful, but overseas readers need one important limitation.

The official repair service states that products being used outside Japan are not eligible for the repair service.

That means the existence of a repair program in Japan should not be read as confirmation that an overseas owner can automatically send a chair into that program.

This distinction matters for Japan Living Edit's audience.

An overseas buyer may still value the fact that the chair is built with replaceable parts, but they should separate that from formal repair eligibility. Before buying, confirm what support, replacement parts, shipping options, and service conditions are actually available for the buyer's location.

Repairability is a product attribute with boundaries.

It is not the same thing as lifetime support, international repair coverage, or a guarantee that every future issue can be solved.

## 8. Does the Nychair X Fit Your Space?

The Nychair X NY-125 is approximately 610 mm wide, 765 mm deep, and 860 mm high when open, with a seat height of approximately 255 mm.

Those dimensions should be evaluated before the word "folding" becomes too persuasive.

Start with the open chair.

Where would it actually sit? Is there room beside a sofa, bed, window, desk, or bookshelf? Can people walk around it? Will the chair interrupt the path between rooms? Does the seat height suit the way the chair would be used?

Then consider the folded chair.

Where would it go when not in use? Would it lean against a wall, fit in a closet, stand near an entryway, or remain visible in the room? Would folding and moving it be something you would actually do, or would it stay open most of the time?

For another way to think about using a compact home vertically rather than only across the floor, see our guide to [using vertical space in a small apartment](/articles/how-to-use-vertical-space-small-apartment/).

The Nychair X may fit some small homes well and others poorly.

The only useful answer comes from measuring the chair against the room.

## 9. Who It May — and May Not — Suit

The Nychair X NY-125 may be worth considering for someone who wants a folding lounge-style chair, values Made-in-Japan production, has room for the open dimensions, and appreciates a product with currently available replacement parts.

It may be less compelling for someone who needs a dining-height chair, wants a chair that can remain outdoors, requires confirmed international repair service, or needs to assess comfort in person before purchase.

It may also be a poor fit if there is nowhere practical to store the chair when folded.

That does not make the chair good or bad in the abstract.

It makes the buying question more specific.

Instead of asking whether the Nychair X is a famous Japanese chair, ask whether the exact NY-125 fits the room, the intended use, the support limitations, and the owner's willingness to manage parts and care over time.
