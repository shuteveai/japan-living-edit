## 1. What Is a Santoku Knife?

Santoku is commonly used for a kitchen knife intended to handle several everyday food-preparation tasks.

KAI states that this 165mm santoku is intended for a broad range of ingredients, including meat, fish, and vegetables. That makes it useful as an example of how the category is positioned: not as a highly specialized single-task knife, but as a general kitchen tool.

That does not mean every santoku suits every cook.

Knife choice depends on cutting style, hand size, board space, food habits, maintenance comfort, and what other knives are already in the kitchen.

For small kitchens especially, the important question is often whether one knife can earn its space by covering the work that is actually repeated. That same space-conscious way of thinking appears in our guide to [Japanese kitchen tools for small kitchens](/articles/japanese-kitchen-tools-small-kitchen/).

## 2. Why 165mm Matters

The KAI Seki Magoroku Damascus Black santoku discussed here has a 165mm blade.

That length is a specification, not a universal recommendation.

A 165mm santoku may be easier to place on a smaller cutting board than a longer chef's knife, but it should still be judged against the user's own kitchen. Someone who regularly breaks down large produce or prefers longer slicing strokes may want a different length. Someone working in a compact apartment kitchen may care more about control within limited counter space.

Before treating 165mm as the right size, compare it with:

- the knife length you currently use most
- the size of your cutting board
- the ingredients you prepare most often
- the amount of working space around the board
- how you prefer to grip and move a knife

The useful role of the measurement is comparison.

It helps a buyer understand whether the knife's size fits the work surface and food habits they already have.
